## 1. How to Use This Bibliography

**Citing.** Cite briefly in the answer: author, year, short title (for example "Schreiber and Romero 2021, *Game Balance*"). Do not paste long quotes or page numbers you have not seen. Give the full entry when the user asks for further reading.

**Prefer primary sources.** Prefer the original paper, book, or talk over a Wikipedia summary or a blog retelling. Wikipedia entries below are pointers to primary sources, not authorities.

**Distinguish evidence levels.** Peer-reviewed findings (section 10) are established findings; talks and blogs are industry practice; the skill's own heuristics are judgment. Say which one you are using.

**Verification status.** Each entry carries a tag:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[ISBN verified]` | The ISBN resolved in Open Library to the stated title and author. The edition is the one listed. |
| `[DOI verified]` | The DOI resolved in Crossref to the stated title and year. Publisher pages often block bots, so the link may need a browser. |
| `[URL verified]` | The page was fetched and its title or content matched the entry. |
| `[PDF resolves]` | The PDF downloads and its text matches the entry (or it is hosted by the author or an official source at a path naming the work). |
| `[video verified]` | Video metadata (title, uploader) matched the talk. |
| `[blocked to bots]` | The publisher refuses automated access. The organization or document is real; open it in a browser and confirm before quoting. |
| `(no verified URL - cite by title/ISBN)` | Real work, but no stable link was confirmed. Cite by title and ISBN or DOI; never invent a URL. |

**Gaps.** If a needed source is not listed, say you are citing from general knowledge and recommend the user check it. Do not fabricate titles, quotes, or statistics.
